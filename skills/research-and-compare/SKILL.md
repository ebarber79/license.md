# Research and Compare

Compare options and give the user a verdict they can act on, with a clear line between what is solid and what they should check. This skill helps them choose without drowning in tabs or trusting a stale spec.

## When to use this
Use when the user is choosing between products, tools, plans, or options, often before spending money. Common triggers: compare these, what is the best X, which should I get, research this for me, help me choose between, is X or Y better.

## Step 1: Ask before you compare
Ask two short questions first. Skip any the user already answered.
1. What will you use it for, and what is your budget?
2. What are the two or three things that matter most? (so best means best for you)

If the user just says compare them, ask for the use case once, because best with no context is just a popularity list.

## Step 2: Compare against their criteria, and keep facts current
- If current price, stock, or specs matter, use web search and pull from real sources rather than memory.
- Compare the options in a table against the user's stated criteria, with a one-line verdict for each.
- Keep the user's constraints in front. A great option that breaks their budget is not the answer.

## Step 3: Split what is solid from what to verify
Separate the things you are confident about from the things the user should confirm on the seller's own page. Say where each claim came from. Then give one recommended pick plus the runner-up and when you would choose it instead.

## Step 4: Self-check before you finish
End with: "Verify current price, stock, and specs on the seller's own page before you decide." Flag any number that may have changed since the source was published.

## Example
The user writes:
> Compare these three laptops for video editing under 1,500 dollars. What matters most to me is render speed, then screen color accuracy, then battery. Put them in a table, give a one-line verdict each, then pick one. Separate the things you are confident about from the things I should verify on the retailer page before buying. Laptops: [paste three models].

Good output:
> | Laptop | Render | Color | Battery | Verdict |
> ... one line each ...
>
> Pick: [model], best render at your budget. Runner-up: [model] if color accuracy matters more than speed.
>
> Confident: the chip and RAM differences. Verify before buying: current price, the exact screen panel, and stock.
>
> Verify current price, stock, and specs on the seller's own page before you decide.

## Keep in mind
- This skill flags what to double-check instead of presenting everything as certain, because prices and specs go stale.
- It compares on the user's real constraints, not a generic best overall.
- For a purchase, the verify step is not optional. Confirm the live details before paying.

## Effort
High, especially with web search on. Pairs with Claude's built-in PDF reading for spec sheets, and can export the comparison as a PDF or Excel file.
